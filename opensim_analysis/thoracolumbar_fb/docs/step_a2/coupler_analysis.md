## 3. Relationship to pelvis_tilt Reserve

**Mechanical mechanism (confirmed by Variation Matrix):**

When couplers are active, the constraint reaction forces act as generalized forces at the pelvis_tilt coordinate through the constraint Jacobian:

```
F_pelvis_tilt += sum_i(dg_i/d(pelvis_tilt)) × lambda_i

For coupler_r: dg/d(pelvis_tilt) = +1.62 → contributes +1.62 × lambda_r
For coupler_l: dg/d(pelvis_tilt) = -1.62 → contributes -1.62 × lambda_l
For elv_angle_r: dg/d(pelvis_tilt) = +2.0 → contributes +2.0 × lambda_elv_r
For elv_angle_l: dg/d(pelvis_tilt) = +2.0 → contributes +2.0 × lambda_elv_l
```

This constraint force provides mechanical equilibrium at the pelvis_tilt DOF without requiring reserve actuation. The net contribution at peak stoop = approximately 174 Nm (measured from Variation Matrix).

**When couplers are removed:** The constraint Jacobian terms vanish. The pelvis_tilt reserve actuator must supply this ~174 Nm to maintain dynamic consistency.

ALL 4 couplers use pelvis_tilt as the independent coordinate. Therefore:
- **All 4 are pelvis_tilt-related**
- Removing ANY of them changes the pelvis_tilt reserve magnitude
- There is no "partial coupler" option that preserves pelvis_tilt balance while freeing shoulder for box motion

This is the fundamental trade-off: the coupler provides pelvis balance at the cost of constraining shoulder motion. For box lifting, free shoulder is required, so the pelvis_tilt reserve anomaly is unavoidable with the no_coupler model.

## 5. Biomechanical Interpretation

The ThoracolumbarFB shoulder rhythm coupler (Ghezelbash et al. 2016) encodes the natural coordination between pelvis tilt and arm elevation during loaded forward bending. The coupling represents:

1. Pendulum compensation: arms rise as trunk bends forward, shifting arm COM backward to partially counterbalance trunk weight.
2. Physiological shoulder rhythm: arm elevation accompanies spinal loading (a documented biomechanical pattern).

Removing this coupler for box motion (where arm position is prescribed by the task, not by spinal kinematics) is biomechanically justified. The pelvis_tilt reserve anomaly is an expected consequence, not a model error.
